# Domain research: David Allen Horizons and Areas of Focus

**Decision this research serves:** Determine what David Allen recommends about Horizons and Areas of Focus, and what that means for Archer.

## Executive summary

David Allen Company's model distinguishes ongoing Areas of Focus (Horizon 2) from finite Projects (Horizon 1); it also describes longer-range goals/objectives, vision, and purpose above them. Its weekly-review guidance says to revisit projects and larger outcomes and keep a current next action on each. This supports a Life Areas/Horizons review surface in Archer, but the reviewed official material does not prescribe a particular page or data model, nor does it say new projects should start Active or Paused. Treat area ownership and project readiness as separate concepts. The exact year bands are approximate and not fully consistent within the 2011 article.

## Horizons model and GTD definitions

David Allen Company presents six levels of focus: Ground (calendar/actions); Horizon 1 (Projects); Horizon 2 (Areas of Focus and Accountability); Horizon 3 (one- to two-year goals and objectives); Horizon 4 (three- to five-year vision); and Horizon 5 (Purpose and Principles). Allen calls the altitude boundaries approximate but useful framing. [1]

The product-relevant distinction is between an ongoing responsibility and a finite outcome. Horizon 2 areas are responsibilities against which a person is accountable; clarifying them can reveal projects to create or retire. Horizon 1 projects are outcomes requiring more than one action step. [1][2] So “Family,” “Career,” “Faith,” and “Learning & Development” fit naturally as Life Areas if they represent ongoing responsibilities. A particular result such as “Complete the teacher certification” is a project, not an area. A goal/outcome can support an area, but Allen’s H3 reference is a longer-range 1–2-year direction, not the name for every outcome in a productivity tool. [1]

**Timeframe caveat:** Source [1]'s headings label H3 as one-to-two-year goals/objectives and H4 as a three-to-five-year vision, but its body examples refer to 12–18 months for H3 and one-to-three years for the larger entity at H4. Allen also calls the altitude divisions approximate. Use the levels to distinguish perspective, not as rigid date boundaries. [1]

## Review practice and software implication

Allen’s official Weekly Review guidance explicitly reviews the Project and Larger Outcome lists, evaluates projects, goals, and outcomes, and checks for at least one current next action on each. [3] The guided review checklist similarly asks for one current action item per project or larger outcome. [4] The broader review includes Get Clear, Get Current, and Get Creative, with Someday/Maybe review under Get Creative. The current official GTD overview also lists frequent system review as the Reflect step. [5]

The published sources support a reviewable hierarchy and suggest Areas should feed into project creation/retirement. They do not prescribe a dedicated Horizons page or a particular app schema. A page for Life Areas is therefore a reasonable product implementation, not a screen Allen specifically mandates. A full six-horizon experience could add value if it helps the user review alignment and identify missing commitments; it should not turn ongoing responsibilities into projects with completion states.

## Recommendation for Archer

Model Life Areas as ongoing records that do not complete, distinct from finite Projects. Goals/outcomes can be linked to Areas; standalone Projects can also be linked directly to an Area when there is no goal. Keep a project’s GTD lifecycle separate: “Paused” is a scheduling/readiness decision, not a different horizon. Based on the official model alone, Allen does not say every new project must start Paused; that is Archer’s deliberate workflow policy.

For a Horizons surface, start with the user's actual reflection need: show Areas, the Goals/outcomes supporting them, and related Projects; make the view useful for review by exposing unsupported/overloaded areas and projects without an active next action. Add H3/H4/H5 content as editable direction/context if the user wants the full model, not as extra project layers. In GTD terms, this is best treated as an explicit Areas/Horizons review surface, not a task dashboard.

**Confidence:** High for the published definitions and weekly-review steps, because these come from official David Allen Company pages. Medium for the proposed Archer information architecture: it is a reasoned application of the framework, not an official product-UI prescription. The research did not establish that a single integrated Horizons page is necessary for GTD correctness.

## Open question

The full model lists H3 as one- to two-year goals/objectives and H4 as three- to five-year vision. Archer's product language and existing Goal workflow should be reviewed separately before mapping its Goals to a specific altitude; a target date alone does not determine the item's conceptual horizon.
