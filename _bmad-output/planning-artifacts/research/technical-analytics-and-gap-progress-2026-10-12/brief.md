# Analytics and Gap Progress Research Brief

**Decision:** Define an analytics and gap-progress epic that distinguishes completed work from demonstrated capability and gives a GTD practitioner useful review feedback.
**Approved plan:** Mthizo requested continuation on 2026-10-12, after the minor-change tracking correction.
**Type:** Technical research, informed by domain and academic-literature lenses.
**Topology:** Three independent perspectives: measurement validity, completion metrics, and product/technical patterns.
**Budget:** Standard preset; three researchers, up to eight sources per dimension per round, at most two rounds. Stop early at coverage or novelty exhaustion. Normal validation; no separate red-team pass.
**Sources:** Direct public-page retrieval and original/official sources. No general web-search tool is available. Do not present this as an exhaustive literature search.
**Output:** Cited Markdown research report and a proposed Epic 9 outline. No runtime implementation or automatic rating changes.

## Questions

1. Does completing a project justify raising a capability rating? What evidence, reassessment, and history should support a claim of gap closure?
2. What exactly should goal/project completion rate mean? How should dates, reopening, archived/parked items, changing scope, and missing history be handled?
3. Which analytics views support review decisions without turning the product into a gamified score? What minimum data/history and accessible presentation patterns are needed?

## Decision Context, Not External Evidence

Archer has user-entered required/current skill ratings, explicit goal/project statuses, guided reviews, and projects linked to goals. The new epic must revise the current no-completion-metrics UX rule while retaining user ownership of self-assessment. These are product constraints, not evidence about the effectiveness of any measurement approach.

## Deliverable Criteria

- Cite actual retrieved evidence; distinguish source findings, local observations, and design judgments.
- Include metric definitions and edge cases, a project-to-gap model, reassessment workflow, and proposed stories.
- Do not fabricate historic completion dates, baseline ratings, weights, or measured capability gains from current statuses.
- Flag limitations and open product decisions before treating a proposed epic as implementation-ready.
