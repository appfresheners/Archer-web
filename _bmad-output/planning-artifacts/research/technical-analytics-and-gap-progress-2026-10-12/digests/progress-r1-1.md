# Progress monitoring and capability measurement

Accessed 2026-10-13. Claims distinguish source findings from design inference.

- **[claim]** Harkin et al. meta-analyzed 138 randomized studies (N=19,951): interventions designed to increase progress monitoring increased monitoring frequency (d+=1.98, 95% CI [1.71, 2.24]) and goal attainment (d+=0.40, 95% CI [0.32, 0.48]); effects were larger when progress was physically recorded or made public. This supports progress monitoring as an aid to goal attainment, not a per-user guarantee or causal claim about any one product feature. **Design inference:** A project's completion is evidence of work being completed, not a measurement of skill-level change. Preserve capability ratings as user-owned assessments; show project/gap progress separately, and offer explicit reassessment where the user can update their rating. Do not automatically increase a rating based on a project status transition.

**Limitations:** Task-learning studies don't establish that every real-world project has the same performance/learning distinction; project completion may still be useful evidence, but it is not sufficient proof of retained capability.
