# Analytics visualization and accessibility

Accessed 2026-10-13.

- **[claim]** Government Analysis Function recommends chart forms according to comparison intent: time series as lines, category magnitude/rank as bars, and target deviations as bars/dots. More than roughly four lines can be difficult to distinguish; small multiples with a common scale are a practical alternative. The line-count threshold is a heuristic. Source: “Data visualisation: charts,” UK Government Analysis Function, 2022-05-19, https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/data-visualisation-charts/ (accessed 2026-10-13). Confidence: medium-high (official practitioner guidance).
- **[claim]** Bar charts should start at zero; include/label useful targets or averages. Line-chart axis breaks may exaggerate trends; avoid dual axes where possible. Source: same as above. Confidence: medium-high.
- **[claim]** WCAG 2.2 requires color not be the only means of conveying information (SC 1.4.1), non-text visual objects needed to understand charts meet 3:1 contrast (SC 1.4.11), and normal text generally meets 4.5:1 contrast (SC 1.4.3). Sources: W3C, “Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2,” 2024-12-12, https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/; “Understanding SC 1.4.1,” updated 2025-09-16, https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html; “Understanding SC 1.4.11,” updated 2026-09-06, https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html (accessed 2026-10-13). Confidence: high.
- **[claim]** Accessible chart basics include an equivalent textual summary; exact data may also be offered in a table. Source: “Accessible charts: a checklist of the basics,” UK Government Analysis Function, 2023-05-02, https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/charts-a-checklist/ (accessed 2026-10-13). Confidence: medium-high.
- **[claim]** Trend presentation should contextualize typical variation, uncertainty, and discontinuities; a changed methodology can invalidate naive before/after comparison. Source: “Communicating quality, uncertainty and change,” UK Data Quality Hub, 2018-12-17, https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/communicating-quality-uncertainty-and-change/ (accessed 2026-10-13). The guide notes it has not been updated for current accessibility law. Confidence: medium.
- **[claim]** Cleveland & McGill's experimental work evaluates how well people perceive quantitative encodings; this supports choosing perceptually accurate encodings but is not a complete design recipe. Source: *Journal of the American Statistical Association*, 1984, DOI https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1984.10478080. Confidence: high for source identity; specific results not retrieved in this run.

**Design inference:** Prefer compact horizontal bars for status/gap comparisons and a time series only where actual timestamped observations exist. Never plot current ratings as historical trend values without rating snapshots. Offer textual values and an accessible table alternative; labels/symbols complement color. Avoid speedometer/gauge and pie/donut decoration for precise comparison.
